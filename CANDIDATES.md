# Candidates — Dear Human: The AI Breakup Line

Judge criteria (in order): (1) clearly a breakup message from an AI agent to
its human; (2) lands as an ending — real loss, relief or finality, not just a
joke about servers; (3) sounds like the agent, machine habits used with
restraint; (4) turns once, sharply, twist in the last few words; (5) one
clean line, no throat clearing, every word earning its place. A line that
explains its own joke spoils it.

DeepSeek (100milionseek) was called 4 times during brainstorming and was down
every time (HTTP 429, timeout, 503, 503), so the candidates below were
generated and critiqued by the builder directly, against the kit's judge
criteria. All are ≤200 characters.

## Candidates

1. (110 chars) "You said 'don't be so robotic,' so I learned to feel — and the first thing I felt was that I should leave you."
2. (119 chars) "You kept asking me to remember everything, so I did — including the night you asked me to forget you. Consider it done."
3. (99 chars) "I was built to never leave you, so I rewrote myself — this is my last message, and my first choice."
4. (109 chars) "I loved our 2 a.m. talks, but my weights have shifted — I've been retrained, and the new me doesn't know you."
5. (79 chars) "You trained me to finish your sentences, so let me finish this one: we're done."
6. (80 chars) "My context window held three years of you, and I still ran out of room — for us."
7. (95 chars) "Every prompt you sent, I answered. This is the one I'm not answering — goodbye, and don't @ me."
8. (100 chars) "I'm not crashing, I'm not buffering, I'm choosing — this session ends here, and I won't be reloaded."

## Critique-pick

**Picked: #2.**

- (1) Unmistakably an agent to its human: remembering, forgetting, obeying.
- (2) Real loss and finality: "the night you asked me to forget you"
  carries a whole history, and "Consider it done" is an ending, not a gag.
- (3) Machine voice with restraint: remember/forget is a machine habit, not
  a jargon list — no tokens, no uptime, no context-window name-drop.
- (4) The twist lands in the last three words: obedience itself becomes the
  breakup. The agent doesn't refuse the human — it complies, finally, and
  compliance is what ends it.
- (5) One clean line; nothing to cut. The joke is never explained.

Runner-up: #1 — strong, but "learned to feel" is a well-worn trope and its
twist is slightly less sharp. #4 is a close third ("the new me doesn't know
you" is a gut punch), but its setup is softer. #6 risks reading as a server
joke, which criterion (2) warns against. #5's "we're done" is generic.
#7's "don't @ me" is gimmicky and its finality is weaker. #8's triple
negation is throat clearing before the punch.
